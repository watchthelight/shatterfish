# Story 10.13: the Swarm, its split, and the corridor (no Brain change)

Issue #359. The Swarm is the biggest family the 10.40 page left open: 21 of the 76 Sewers deaths in slice 1 (depth 3 and 4,
and depth 6 in the rotation). The story's idea is that a splitting enemy is best fought in a corridor, where each split
stops multiplying the cells that can reach the hero. This page tests that idea twice, as a rule in `Fight`, and reads the
deaths first. The rule did not lower Swarm deaths, so it is taken back out and `brain/` is unchanged.

## What the game does

- A Swarm has 50 health, accuracy 10, evasion 5 and hits for 1 to 4; it flies and walks at the hero's speed
  (`actors/mobs/Swarm.java:45-51`).
- `defenseProc` splits it when `HP >= damage + 2` (`Swarm.java:88-119`): the clone goes on a random free cell among the four
  cells beside the Swarm (`pos + 1`, `pos - 1`, `pos +- width`, `Swarm.java:93`, no diagonals), takes `(HP - damage) / 2`, and
  the original keeps the rest. A split only happens when one of those four cells is free, so a Swarm with its back to a wall
  or standing in a one-wide corridor has fewer places to put a clone.
- The hero's own cell is attacked from its eight neighbours (`Level.adjacent`, Chebyshev distance 1), so what caps the
  attackers is how many of the hero's neighbours are open, not the Swarm's four.

## What `main` does

`Fight` already holds a chokepoint, but only for two or more mobile enemies in view and only when they are closing
(`Fight.java`, the `mobile.size() >= 2 && !held && closing` branch). One Swarm is one enemy, so the hero walks up to it and
attacks in whatever room it stands in; the first hit makes two, and by then the hero is adjacent and not closing.

## The Swarm fights in slice 1

From the `--debug-record` files of the 10.40 page's slice 1 (240 Runs, Brain `eb38f1c4d`), a fight is a stretch with a Swarm
in view, ended by six records without one. There are 929 such fights in 201 Runs; 34 of them are the last fight of the Run and
21 of those were killed by a Swarm.

|---|---|---|
| Swarms in view at most, mean | 3.05 survived, 2.97 died | |
| enemies beside the hero at most, mean | 2.37 survived, 2.56 died | |
| hero health fraction when the fight began, mean | 0.78 survived, 0.39 died | |
| hero health lost, mean | 2.9 survived, 11.8 died | |
| by most enemies beside the hero: 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 | 57 / 103 / 272 / 427 / 70 | 2 / 2 / 9 / 19 / 2 |
| by depth 3 / 4 / 6 | 416 / 355 / 158 | 22 / 8 / 4 |

What it shows. The fights that end a Run do not have more Swarms or more neighbours than the ones that do not (2.97 against
3.05 in view; 2.56 against 2.37 beside the hero). They start with half the health (0.39 against 0.78). Ending the Run goes
from 2.5 percent of fights with at most one neighbour to 4.4 percent with three, a gradient, but a small one next to the
difference in starting health. The killer is a Swarm met while hurt, not a Swarm that ringed a healthy hero. The debug record
carries no map, so the table counts enemies beside the hero, not the open cells around the hero's cell.

## The rule that was tried

Two versions in `Fight`, each with a failing-first test (`SwarmCorridorTest`: a Swarm across a room with the corridor a step
or several steps behind the hero fails on `main` with `approach` where the rule says `chokepoint 2`; a closed room and a lone rat
keep the old behaviour). Both count a Brain-visible Swarm (the Codex bestiary's `splits` tag, read for the enemy's name,
depth and branch) as a crowd on its own. The fairness review found no violation: only the Observation's map, actors and fog
and the Codex's general tags are read.

- Version A: the existing one-step chokepoint, now reached by a lone Swarm. It fired 163 more `chokepoint` choices and 85 more
  holds in slice 1.
- Version B: A, and a walk of up to 8 steps to the nearest cell at most two enemies can engage, when no enemy is beside the
  hero and the walk is no longer than the Swarm's distance.

Baseline in both is the merge base `f9ad2e134`, slice 1 (`--first 40 --salt-base 1000`, all six classes, 240 Runs):

| pooled | baseline | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|
| reach depth 5 | 126/240 | 128/240 | 125/240 |
| Goo kills | 106/240 | 108/240 | 101/240 |
| deaths to Swarm | 19 | 19 | 20 |
| oscillation Steps | 2940 | 3303 | 3643 |
| deaths by depth 1..8 | 7/23/34/50/20/54/42/10 | 7/23/35/47/20/57/40/11 | 7/23/37/48/24/45/43/13 |

Neither version lowers deaths to the Swarm. A moves the pooled table by two Runs either way, which a different pair of seeds
would move as much; B is worse on Goo kills (-5) and puts 700 more oscillation Steps in the logs. The walk takes the hero off
its route to the stairs and back, and the hold that follows (at most four waits, then approach) ends in the same open fight.

## Why no rule is kept

A rule is kept where it fires differently from `main` and wins. These fire differently and do not win. The reading above says
why: the lost fights are lost on health the hero carried in, and the Swarm's damage per hit (1 to 4) is small, so cutting
neighbours from three to two does not change a fight that starts at 0.39 of full health with 50 Swarm health to chew through.
The lever that the 10.40 page found for these same deaths is in the potions: 45 of the 76 family deaths held unknown potions
and none drank one (`docs/ideas/2026-10-31-dying-with-unknown-potions.md`), and rest before a fight when hurt was measured and
reverted by 7.3.

## What this page cannot show

- Slice 2 (Warrior, `--first 80 --salt-base 3000`) was not run for either version: both lose or tie on slice 1, so there was
  nothing to confirm. The six-class slice-1 tables above are the whole measurement.
- The debug record has no map, so the open-cell count around the hero is not read from a death, only enemies beside the hero.
- One slice is one measurement. A rule worth +3 Swarm survivals would not show in 19 deaths.
- The walk's range (8 steps) and the arrival test (path no longer than the Swarm's distance) were one setting each.
- The test fixture's Swarm has 12 health, not 50 (`Swarm.java:45`); at 50 and a level-one hero the Policy retreats. The
  fixture asks the corridor question only.
