# Story 10.9: the DM-100 deaths read one by one; a cover rule measured and not kept

Issue #355. The DM-100 is the top single killer on depth 7 in the W1 tables. This page reads every DM-100 death of the 240-Run slice, says what the evidence supports, and records the one rule that was built against it and then taken out because it moved nothing.

## The slice

`./gradlew :rig:direction --args="--set standard --class ALL --first 40 --salt-base 1000 --debug-record"` on the Brain of `origin/e10/instruments` (240 Runs, six classes). Killers are read from each Run's `.ending.json`, the last Decision from its `.debug.ndjson`.

DM-100 was the killer in 8 of 240 Runs (3.3%), all on depth 7, all after Goo was dead.

| class | seed | last HP | hunger on the last Decision | what the last Decisions show |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cleric | ...3f0 | 1 | starving | fighting beside it, then exploring at 1 HP |
| Huntress | ...3ee | 5 | starving | bow at 2 cells, then fighting at 1-2 cells |
| Mage | ...3ec | 4 | starving | staff zaps and melee beside it at 4-5 HP |
| Warrior | ...3fc | 4 | starving | two DM-100s, one adjacent and one at 4 cells; 30 to 4 HP |
| Warrior | ...405 | 3 | starving | "fight" and "retreat: stairs" along the line at 4-6 cells; 19 to 3 HP |
| Warrior | ...3ef | 3 | not hungry | two DM-100s, one adjacent and one at 3-4 cells; 31 to 3 HP |
| Warrior | ...3eb | 5 | starving | approach and descend back and forth at 5-6 cells; 13 to 5 HP |
| Warrior | ...40b | 11 | hungry | two DM-100s, one adjacent and one at 4 cells; 36 to 11 HP |

Five of the eight heroes were starving, one hungry. A starving hero does not regenerate (`regen-rest-explore`), so these are Runs the hunger story (10.15) owns first: the DM-100 lands the last blow on a hero that could not heal back from the zaps before it. Three Runs, not eight, have a clear DM-100 pattern a rule could touch: two of them (the 405 and 3eb Warriors) stand or step along the bolt line at 4 to 6 cells, and the fed ones (3ef, 40b) fight one DM-100 in melee while a second zaps from 3 to 4 cells.

## What was built and measured

A `cover` Policy above the missile Policy: when a DM-100 had a clear bolt line to the hero, nothing was adjacent, and a cell within three steps had no line to it (and was nearer than charging), step there; out of view with the DM-100 seen in the last five waits, wait for it to come round the corner (Mob.java:1365-1395: a hunter that has lost sight walks to where it last saw the hero). A starving hero was left to the old Policies. It had a CoachLine sentence, a Rule row, and a test that failed without it.

The same 240 Runs with the Policy on, against the same Brain without it:

| | without | with |
|---|---|---|
| reach depth 5, pooled | 102/240 | 102/240 |
| reach depth 10, pooled | 0/240 | 0/240 |
| Goo kills, pooled | 72/240 | 72/240 |
| DM-100 deaths | 8 | 8 |
| Skeleton deaths | 30 | 31 |
| deepest mean, Warrior | 5.875 | 5.900 |

Five classes' rows were identical column for column; only the Warrior row moved by one Run (one Warrior reached depth 8 instead of dying on 7). The Policy changed no outcome that a table can see, so the second slice (80 Warriors, salt base 3000) was not spent on it.

## Verdict

No rule kept. Eight deaths in 240 leave at most a few Runs for any DM-100 rule to save, five of them starving, and depth 10 is out of reach for every class whatever happens on depth 7. The cover rule is a plausible fix for the two line-standing Warriors but it does not show in the numbers, and an unmeasured Policy is cost with no benefit. What the evidence does support:

- Fix hunger first (story 10.15); it explains most of this killer and the Skeleton one beside it.
- Reopen a DM-100 rule only when a slice has enough fed-hero DM-100 deaths to measure. The one pattern seen in fed heroes is two DM-100s at once (one in melee, one zapping): a rule for that is a fight-estimate change, in the Fight Policy's threat estimate, and belongs with the Skeleton work, not in a new Policy.
