### (a) How Fishtest runs SPRT today

- CLAIM: Fishtest does not run a textbook Wald SPRT; it runs a Generalized SPRT (GSPRT) in which unknown nuisance parameters are replaced by maximum-likelihood estimates, and its documented reference for the math is Michel Van den Bergh's notes hosted at cantate.be/Fishtest/ (archived at archive.org/details/fishtest_mathematics/), with validation code at github.com/vdbergh/simul and github.com/vdbergh/pentanomial. | SOURCE: https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html | PUBLISHER: Stockfish project (official docs mirror of the fishtest wiki) | PUB_DATE: unknown (page undated; fetched 2026-09-03) | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: The GSPRT statistic is LLR := L(theta1_hat, x) - L(theta0_hat, x) where theta_i_hat is the MLE constrained to phi(theta)=phi_i, and it stops when LLR leaves [log(beta/(1-alpha)), log((1-beta)/alpha)], exactly like Wald's SPRT. | SOURCE: http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf ("A practical introduction to the GSPRT", section 1) | PUBLISHER: Michel Van den Bergh (fishtest statistician) | PUB_DATE: unknown (undated PDF) | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Van den Bergh's approximation (2.1) makes the GSPRT trivial to compute: LLR ~= (1/2) * (phi1 - phi0) * (2*phi_hat - phi0 - phi1) / V(phi_hat), where phi_hat is the unconstrained MLE of the quantity of interest (e.g. expected score) and V(phi_hat) is any O(1/sqrt N)-accurate estimator of its variance; for multinomial outcomes with payoffs a_i: phi_hat = sum a_i p_i_hat and V(phi_hat) = (1/N) * (sum a_i^2 p_i_hat - phi_hat^2). | SOURCE: http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf (section 2, eq. 2.1 and example) | PUBLISHER: Michel Van den Bergh | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: The fishtest wiki describes this same formula as treating "the GSPRT like an SPRT for the drift of a Brownian motion, where the infinitesimal variance is estimated from the sample data" (i.e. the "SPRT with unknown variance" view); Elo estimates come from formula (6.1) of the Brownian-approximation note, and expected durations come from sprta.pdf. | SOURCE: https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html | PUBLISHER: Stockfish project | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Fishtest uses the pentanomial model: a trial is a game PAIR played with reversed colours, with 5 outcomes (LL, LD+DL, LW+DD+WL, DW+WD, WW) and payoffs a = (1, 3/4, 1/2, 1/4, 0); N is games/2; the wiki says this yields "a substantial saving of testing resources" over the trinomial model because it captures the correlation between the two games of a pair. | SOURCE: http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf (section 2 example) and https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html | PUBLISHER: Michel Van den Bergh / Stockfish project | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: The live implementation is server/fishtest/stats/sprt.py (class `sprt`, constructor takes alpha, beta, elo0, elo1, elo_model in {"logistic","normalized"}; bounds `a = log(beta/(1-alpha))`, `b = log((1-beta)/alpha)`; `self.llr = N * mu_LLR` with mu_LLR from `LLR_drift_variance()`; LLR is clamped if it exceeds 1.03x a bound) and server/fishtest/stats/LLRcalc.py (`L_(x) = 1/(1+10**(-x/400))`, `LLR_logistic(elo0, elo1, results)`, `LLR_normalized(nelo0, nelo1, results)`, `results_to_pdf`, `stats(pdf)` returning mean and variance; results of length 3 are trinomial, length 5 are pentanomial pair frequencies). | SOURCE: https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/blob/master/server/fishtest/stats/sprt.py and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/master/server/fishtest/stats/LLRcalc.py | PUBLISHER: official-stockfish/fishtest (GitHub, master branch) | PUB_DATE: unknown (master as of 2026-09-03) | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: ecosystem

### (b) SPRT for a Bernoulli proportion

- CLAIM: Wald SPRT: with Z_i = log f1(X_i)/f0(X_i), accumulate S_n = sum Z_i and stop when S_n <= log(beta/(1-alpha)) (accept H0) or S_n >= log((1-beta)/alpha) (accept H1); Wald's approximations: E0[N] ~= [alpha*log((1-beta)/alpha) + (1-alpha)*log(beta/(1-alpha))] / E0[Z_i] and E1[N] ~= [(1-beta)*log((1-beta)/alpha) + beta*log(beta/(1-alpha))] / E1[Z_i], from Wald's identity E[Z]E[N] = E[S_N]. | SOURCE: https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Sequential_probability_ratio_test | PUBLISHER: Encyclopedia of Mathematics (Springer/EMS) | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: For Bernoulli data the per-run LLR increments are: win -> log(p1/p0); loss -> log((1-p1)/(1-p0)). For p0=0.05, p1=0.07 (a "5% -> 7% win rate" hypothesis pair): win step = log(1.4) = +0.3365, loss step = log(0.93/0.95) = -0.02128; E0[Z] = 0.05*0.3365 - 0.95*0.02128 = -0.00339 per run; E1[Z] = 0.07*0.3365 - 0.93*0.02128 = +0.00377 per run. | SOURCE: computed by me from the LLR definition in https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Sequential_probability_ratio_test | PUBLISHER: n/a (own arithmetic) | PUB_DATE: 2026-09-03 | CONFIDENCE: high (arithmetic), formula sourced | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: With alpha=beta=0.05 the bounds are +-log(19) = +-2.944, and Wald's approximation gives expected sample sizes E0[N] ~= 2.650/0.00339 ~= 780 runs when the true win rate is 5%, and E1[N] ~= 2.650/0.00377 ~= 700 runs when it is 7%; a fixed-sample one-sample binomial test for the same alpha/beta needs n ~= [(1.645*sqrt(0.05*0.95) + 1.645*sqrt(0.07*0.93))/0.02]^2 ~= 1500 runs, so SPRT saves roughly half, consistent with the classical result. These numbers assume p0 is KNOWN (one-sample test against a fixed baseline). | SOURCE: computed by me from formulas in https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Sequential_probability_ratio_test | PUBLISHER: n/a (own arithmetic) | PUB_DATE: 2026-09-03 | CONFIDENCE: medium (Wald approximations ignore overshoot; single source class -- needs a second source before being quoted as a design number) | CLASS: performance
- CLAIM: For the two-bot case where both win rates are unknown, use the GSPRT form with phi = pA - pB, V(phi_hat) = pA_hat qA_hat / nA + pB_hat qB_hat / nB; with equal arms the variance of phi_hat is ~2x the one-sample case, so expected runs per arm roughly double (roughly 4x total games) relative to the numbers above. With paired seeds, instead test phi = E[d_i] where d_i = winA_i - winB_i in {-1,0,+1} on seed i and V = var(d)/N; if the two bots' outcomes on the same seed are positively correlated, var(d) < pA qA + pB qB and the test is shorter -- this is the direct analogue of fishtest's pentanomial pairing. | SOURCE: derived from eq. (2.1) and the multinomial example in http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf | PUBLISHER: Michel Van den Bergh (formula); derivation mine | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: medium | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Futility near p ~= 0: (i) the loss step log((1-p1)/(1-p0)) -> 0 as p0,p1 -> 0 while the win step log(p1/p0) stays large, so at p0=0.01, p1=0.03 one win contributes +1.099 (37% of the 2.944 bound) and a handful of lucky wins can end the test -- Wald's E[N] approximations (which give E0[N] ~= 290, E1[N] ~= 200 here) become unreliable because excess-over-boundary (overshoot) is no longer negligible; (ii) in the GSPRT/normalized form V(phi_hat) = p_hat q_hat / N is zero when no wins have been observed, so the statistic is undefined until the first win -- fishtest handles this by `regularize(results)` (pseudo-counts) inside `results_to_pdf`. | SOURCE: own arithmetic from https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Sequential_probability_ratio_test ; overshoot lead: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16076v3 ("Improving Wald's (approximate) SPRT by avoiding overshoot", not fetched) ; `regularize` seen in https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/master/server/fishtest/stats/LLRcalc.py | PUBLISHER: mixed | PUB_DATE: 2024-10 (arXiv) / unknown | CONFIDENCE: medium (overshoot paper not read this run) | CLASS: failure

### (c) Sequential tests for ordinal / continuous outcomes

- CLAIM: The GSPRT approximation is metric-agnostic: for ANY quantity phi with an MLE phi_hat and variance estimate V(phi_hat), LLR ~= (1/2)(phi1-phi0)(2 phi_hat - phi0 - phi1)/V(phi_hat). For depth reached (or turns survived) treated as numeric, phi = mean depth, phi_hat = sample mean, V = s^2/N; this is a sequential t-test-like statistic and is exactly what fishtest already does for the 5-valued pair score. | SOURCE: http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf (eq. 2.1; the paper states "any standard estimator will do" for V) | PUBLISHER: Michel Van den Bergh | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Under H1 the per-observation LLR drift of that statistic is (phi1-phi0)^2 / (2 sigma^2), so E1[N] ~= 2.650 * 2 sigma^2 / (phi1-phi0)^2 at alpha=beta=0.05. Worked example: if depth has sd ~= 4 floors and the target effect is +0.3 floors, E1[N] ~= 2.650*32/0.09 ~= 940 unpaired runs per bot; if paired seeds cut the sd of the per-seed difference to ~2.5, E1[N] ~= 2.650*12.5/0.09 ~= 370 seed pairs. (sd values are illustrative placeholders, not measured.) | SOURCE: own derivation from eq. (2.1) in http://cantate.be/Fishtest/GSPRT_approximation.pdf and Wald's identity in https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Sequential_probability_ratio_test | PUBLISHER: n/a | PUB_DATE: 2026-09-03 | CONFIDENCE: medium (formula sourced; numbers illustrative) | CLASS: performance

Versions: v1 2015-12-15, v2 2016-02-17, v3 2019-07-16. | SOURCE: https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922 ; https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/opre.2021.2135 (not fetched) ; secondary: https://blog.analytics-toolkit.com/2022/comparison-of-the-statistical-power-of-sequential-tests/ | PUBLISHER: arXiv / INFORMS / Analytics-Toolkit | PUB_DATE: 2015-12 to 2022 | CONFIDENCE: high for existence/framing, medium for formula details (abstract only fetched) | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: mSPRT trades a fixed alternative for a mixing distribution and therefore "performs well even when the exact alternative is unknown" -- relevant when the true improvement size is unknown, which is the Shatterfish situation; the cost is lower power at a given N than an SPRT with the correctly guessed alternative. | SOURCE: WebSearch summaries of https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.04922 and https://blog.analytics-toolkit.com/2022/comparison-of-the-statistical-power-of-sequential-tests/ | PUBLISHER: arXiv / Analytics-Toolkit | PUB_DATE: 2015-2022 | CONFIDENCE: medium | CLASS: pattern

### (e) Picking bounds and effect sizes when baseline is near zero

- CLAIM: Fishtest's design choice to state bounds in normalized (standardized) units exists precisely so that expected test length depends only on the bounds and not on the outcome distribution; for Shatterfish this argues for stating hypotheses in standardized effect units (e.g. "phi1 - phi0 = 0.1 sd of the paired-difference metric") rather than raw win-rate points, so the same bounds give the same run budget across metrics. | SOURCE: https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/fishtest-wiki/Fishtest-Mathematics.html | PUBLISHER: Stockfish project | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: medium (inference from a documented design rationale) | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: Fishtest's two-stage pattern (a permissive, cheap STC bound such as [0, 2] then a tighter, expensive LTC bound such as [0, 1]) plus a separate non-regression bound shape ([-x, +small]) for simplifications is the field's answer to "small but real improvement": H0 at (or just below) zero, H1 at the smallest effect worth the compute; issue #764 documents that when outcomes become less informative (draw rate 75% -> 90%) the bounds must be tightened to keep the pass-through rate meaningful -- the same logic applies when win rate is near zero (each run carries little information). | SOURCE: https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/issues/764 | PUBLISHER: fishtest issue tracker | PUB_DATE: 2020-08-17 | CONFIDENCE: medium | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: Because per-run information in a Bernoulli outcome scales with p(1-p) (~0.05 at p=5% vs 0.25 at p=50%), while depth (1-26 ordinal) or turns survived carry information on every run, a GSPRT on the paired-seed depth difference will reach a verdict in far fewer runs than a win-rate SPRT at the same standardized bounds; win rate should be reported (with a CI from the same GSPRT machinery, cf. `lower_cb` in sprt.py) but not used as the gating test until p is well above zero. | SOURCE: derived from eq. ## Contradictions
- Bounds: the brief cites [0, 2] and [-1.75, 0.25]; issue #764 (2020) shows historical logistic bounds <-0.5,1.5>/<0.25,1.75> and proposals of [0.25,1.25]; an aggregator snippet says current STC [0,2.0] / LTC [0,1.0] nElo. These are not contradictory so much as different eras/units (logistic Elo pre-2020 vs normalized Elo after); the current primary default was not verified this run.
- Sample size for 5%->7%: my Wald one-sample estimate (~700-780 runs) vs the two-sample reality (~2-4x more). Any ADR number must say which design it assumes.
- Miller's random-walk test ignores failures and is presented as good for low conversion; the Wald/GSPRT view says near-zero p makes per-run information tiny and overshoot large. Both are true: Miller's test still needs N sized from baseline rate and lift, and that N grows as p -> 0.
