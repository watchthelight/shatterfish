### Lead 1: Long, Sturtevant, Buro, Furtak 2010 (PIMC predictors) - read from the PDF text this run
- CLAIM: Leaf correlation lc is defined as "the probability all sibling, terminal nodes have the same payoff value"; low lc means a player can still change their payoff very late in the game (Sec. "Properties of Game Trees"). | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 proceedings (Univ. of Alberta author copy) | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Bias b is "the probability that the game will favor a particular player over the other"; in the synthetic model each correlated leaf pair is set to +1 with probability b and -1 otherwise, so b=0.5 is unbiased and b=1 means one side always wins (Sec. "Properties of Game Trees" and "Synthetic Trees"). | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Disambiguation factor df "determines how quickly the number of nodes in a player's information set shrinks with regard to the depth of the tree"; generatively, each time player p moves, each of p's information sets is split in half with probability df (recursively); df=0 means hidden info is never revealed (poker), df=1 collapses to perfect information. | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: The paper's measurement recipe for real games is: run random playouts to terminal nodes; estimate lc and b from the local neighbourhood of sibling leaves (they use "pre-terminal nodes" and walk down the tree avoiding moves that end the game early); estimate df by computing the size of the current player's consistent information set at a node and again the next time that player moves, then convert the average reduction ratio to df. In Skat they measured 10,000 human-bid deals, 1,000 lc/b samples per deal near the leaves, and only 10 disambiguation rollouts per world because df clustered tightly at ~0.6. | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: The three conditions under which PIMC (and thus strategy fusion) does NOT hurt much are, from the synthetic-tree results: (1) high leaf correlation (PIMC is "at its worst when leaf node correlation is low", because it believes critical decisions come "later"); (2) extreme bias (effect "generally small", helps when correlation is medium/low); (3) high disambiguation factor (as df approaches 1 the game approaches perfect information and PIMC "improves drastically"; in the 0.7-0.9 df range even low correlation becomes good for PIMC). Mid-range df is the worst case for PIMC's absolute score. | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math
- CLAIM: Calibration points: Skat and Hearts measured lc 0.8-1.0, df ~0.6, bias varying by hand, placing them where PIMC loses only ~0.1 points/game vs a Nash equilibrium and gains ~0.4 over random (payoffs in [-1,1]); Kuhn poker has df=0, lc=0.5, b=0.5, where PIMC is predicted to be poor and empirically is exploitable by a best-response opponent. Experimental setup: depth-8 synthetic trees, 8 worlds per player (64 at the chance node), 10,000 trees per parameter triple, 2 games per tree, opponent = CFR Nash player. | SOURCE: https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI-10 | PUB_DATE: 2010-07 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: performance
- CLAIM (my derivation, not stated by any paper): for a dungeon crawler, lc/b/df can be measured with the same recipe using the bot's own simulator: sample belief-consistent worlds, random-play to a horizon (death/kill/escape as terminal), compare sibling-action outcomes at the last decision to estimate lc; estimate b as the fraction of correlated outcomes that are wins for the given encounter; estimate df as the ratio of consistent-world count (belief particles surviving) between successive hero turns. Roguelike combat likely has high df (each monster turn reveals monster type, damage rolls, and identities of used items) and moderately high lc late in a fight (once HP margins are decided), which by Long et al.'s criteria predicts plain redetermination is not badly hurt; the poker-like regime (df~0) applies to unidentified-item decisions, not to tactics. | SOURCE: derived from https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~nathanst/papers/pimc.pdf | PUBLISHER: n/a | PUB_DATE: unknown | CONFIDENCE: low | CLASS: pattern

The map itself is deliberately excluded from the state. | SOURCE: https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12923/12923-52-16440-1-2-20201228.pdf | PUBLISHER: AAAI (AIIDE-17 proceedings) | PUB_DATE: 2017-10 | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: pattern
- CLAIM: Their actions are abstract: 9 primitives (approach, line up, melee, etc.) plus one action per inventory item whose semantics depend on item type; a per-action prerequisite function masks illegal actions in both the policy and replay updates. Training: 1M actions (weapons-only) / 2M actions (full items), replay buffer 1M, gamma 0.9, network 128-64 dense, Adam lr 0.001; full-items model took ~10 hours. Evaluation: 12 monsters at levels 14-17 in an 8x16 arena with starvation/weight/identification disabled; DQN beats the scripted baseline on weapons-only but the full-items model is "the same or lower". ## Contradictions
- ISMCTS 2012 vs itself across games: in Dou Di Zhu the determinizations-vs-iterations split barely matters, in LOTR:C fewer determinizations with deeper per-determinization search wins by 22.9%. Whitehouse 2014 resolves this as game-dependent ("sensitive to the ratio"), so Shatterfish must run its own split sweep rather than copy 40x250.
- Long et al. 2010: low disambiguation is good for PIMC's absolute score but bad for PIMC's gain over random; the relevant metric for an ADR is gain over a cheap baseline, where high df wins.
- Round-1 framing that Campbell and Verbrugge built an abstract tactical model: they built an abstract state/action encoding for RL; the transition model is the real game. No hand-built roguelike combat simulator with accuracy/speed numbers was found.
