# Red-team: symbolic-first brain architecture (priority list + belief tables + redetermination search, learned components late/optional)

Scope note: skeptic received only the conclusion text. Every claim below cites a source retrieved on 2026-09-03. Two fetched PDFs (Long et al. 2010; dcss-ai-wrapper PRL 2021) came back as unreadable binary and are therefore listed under "Searched and not found" rather than cited for content.

## Counter-evidence

- FINDING: LLM agents on NetHack went from ~1.6% BALROG progression (o1-preview, late 2024) to 40.0 +/- 7.7% (Gemini-3-Pro, entry dated 2026-02-03), with Claude-Opus-4.5-Thinking at 30.0% and Gemini-3.1-Pro at 35.0%, all in roughly 15 months with zero game-specific engineering. "Learned components are late and optional" assumes the learned frontier is static; it is moving an order of magnitude per year on exactly this game class. | SOURCE: https://balrogai.com/ | PUBLISHER: BALROG leaderboard (balrog-ai) | PUB_DATE: entries dated 2025-04 to 2026-02 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: The BALROG paper (ICLR 2025) reported that top LLMs "completely fail to make meaningful progress" on NetHack (o1-preview 1.57%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 1.16%, most models 0%). This is the baseline the 2026 leaderboard numbers must be read against; it establishes the slope, not a plateau. | SOURCE: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543 | PUBLISHER: arXiv / ICLR 2025 | PUB_DATE: 2024-11 (v2), ICLR 2025 | STRENGTH: weak (alone it supports the conclusion; it matters only as the start point of the trend)
- FINDING: An independent 2026 harness (Python-sandbox API, observation masking, memory tools, sliding context) got GPT-5.2 to dungeon level 10 (mean DL 3.5 over 37 runs) and Gemini-3-Flash to DL 9. Author's stated conclusion: "the harness can be just as important as the model"; spatial awareness was the dominant failure. This is direct evidence that a hybrid (symbolic scaffold + learned policy) is a live design in 2026, not a late option. It also confirms LLMs still trail the 2021 symbolic bots ("the top symbolic agent beat the top neural agent by a factor of almost 3"). | SOURCE: https://kenforthewin.github.io/blog/posts/nethack-agent/ | PUBLISHER: Vaguely Aligned (personal blog) | PUB_DATE: 2026-01-30 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: No agent of any kind, symbolic included, has ascended modern NetHack (3.6.6+). The Manifold market resolved YES only on a 2015 bot that used pudding farming, an exploit removed in 3.6.0; the creator noted "no previous work had accomplished ascension on the latest version". The symbolic champion (AutoAscend) is itself plateaued well short of winning, so "symbolic-first" is a route to a mid-dungeon plateau, not to completing the game. | SOURCE: https://manifold.markets/NLeseul/will-an-artificial-agent-ascend-in | PUBLISHER: Manifold Markets | PUB_DATE: resolved 2024-02-08 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: "NetHack is Hard to Hack" (NeurIPS 2023) had to augment AutoAscend's codebase to extract hierarchical strategy labels and build a 10^9-action dataset; it describes AutoAscend as relying on "a large network of handcrafted rules" whose global controller switches strategies "in an imperative manner via sets of strategy-specific predicates". The cost of the priority-list design shows up as (a) a strategy stack opaque enough that a research team needed instrumentation to recover what it was doing, and (b) learned imitators trained on it still lag, meaning the rules encode expertise that is expensive to author and hard to transfer. | SOURCE: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/764ba7236fb63743014fafbd87dd4f0e-Paper-Conference.pdf | PUBLISHER: NeurIPS 2023 | PUB_DATE: 2023-12 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: Behaviour trees (the AutoAscend arbitration model is a BT-like hierarchy of strategies with entry predicates) "can become intractable in large-scale games owing to the exponential escalation of tree complexity that arises from the combinatorial growth of behavioral elements", and their "inherent static nature ... constrains their adaptability to dynamic scenarios that were not anticipated". The paper proposes goal-oriented plus utility-based planning inside BTs (GOBT) as the fix. | SOURCE: https://www.jmis.org/archive/view_article?pid=jmis-10-4-321 | PUBLISHER: Journal of Multimedia Information System, vol 10 no 4 | PUB_DATE: 2023-12 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: Game-AI practitioner literature characterises BTs as offering "more control but less problem solving" compared with planners such as GOAP, and reports that studios "combine methods" rather than shipping a single priority hierarchy. A pure priority list is the low-problem-solving end of that spectrum for a game whose difficulty is problem solving under uncertainty. | SOURCE: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/behavior-trees-and-the-future-of-intelligent-control-2 | PUBLISHER: Game Developer | PUB_DATE: undated on retrieval | STRENGTH: weak
- FINDING: The ISMCTS paper defines strategy fusion as occurring "whenever an algorithm combines strategies determined for various worlds into a single optimal strategy" and shows determinization also suffers non-locality; it introduces Information Set MCTS precisely because plain determinization "considers the game as a perfect-information one". The conclusion's plan (sample hidden state, then search on the real engine) is exactly the PIMC pattern this work was written to replace, and the conclusion offers no roguelike measurement showing the pattern is safe here. | SOURCE: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf | PUBLISHER: IEEE TCIAIG (Cowling, Powley, Whitehouse) | PUB_DATE: 2012 | STRENGTH: moderate
- FINDING: The "high disambiguation / high leaf correlation makes PIMC fine" argument comes from Long, Sturtevant, Buro and Furtak, who derived those properties from synthetic game trees and validated them on two-player trick-taking card games. The paper's own framing is that these properties must be "detected in real games"; the conclusion asserts values for roguelikes ("expected to have") without any such detection. Roguelike item identification is the opposite of trick-taking disambiguation: an unidentified wand or scroll stays ambiguous for many turns and may only disambiguate by being used (an irreversible, potentially lethal action), which is the low-disambiguation regime the paper flags as bad for PIMC. | SOURCE: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7562 | PUBLISHER: AAAI 2010 (abstract page; full PDF fetched but unreadable this run) | PUB_DATE: 2010 | STRENGTH: moderate (argument from the paper's stated method; roguelike values not measured either way)
- FINDING: Recent benchmark work states that "traditional game agents often assume privileged interfaces - direct APIs to game state or unimodal inputs - that bypass many challenges human players face", and that humans "must ground actions in raw pixels, resolve UI affordances ... all under partial observability". The NLE observation space (glyph IDs 0..5991, blstats vector) is itself a privileged structured interface, so AutoAscend's success is evidence about a bot with a clean semantic feed, not about a bot restricted to what a human sees on screen. The conclusion's observation-only rule removes the exact input that made the cited reference architecture cheap. | SOURCE: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18483 | PUBLISHER: arXiv (StarBench) | PUB_DATE: 2025-10 | STRENGTH: moderate

## Strongest argument the conclusion is wrong

The conclusion generalises from one data point (AutoAscend, NeurIPS 2021) to a design rule for 2026 and beyond, and the data point has three problems. First, its input was the NLE's structured glyph/blstats feed, a privileged semantic interface, not a human-visible screen (StarBench, 2025; NLE observation spec); the observation-only constraint removes the very thing that made the priority list cheap to write, so entry predicates will have to sit on top of a perception/belief layer that AutoAscend never had to build. Second, the symbolic champion is itself plateaued: no bot of any architecture has ascended NetHack 3.6.6+ (Manifold, 2024), and the research community had to instrument AutoAscend's rule network just to understand what it does (NeurIPS 2023), which is the maintenance and opacity cost that BT literature warns becomes "intractable" as behaviours multiply (JMIS, 2023). Third, the learned frontier is not static: NetHack progression by zero-engineering LLM agents went from about 1.6% to 40% between late 2024 and February 2026 (BALROG paper; balrogai.com leaderboard), and a 2026 independent harness found "the harness can be just as important as the model", i.e. the winning shape in 2026 is hybrid: symbolic scaffolding, memory and observation masking around a learned policy, not symbolic-first with learning deferred. Add that the search plan is textbook PIMC, whose failure modes (strategy fusion, non-locality) motivated ISMCTS (Cowling et al., 2012), and that the "high disambiguation" claim is asserted rather than measured despite roguelike item-ID being a low-disambiguation, use-to-learn regime (Long et al., 2010 method). A team following this design risks spending six months hand-writing predicates against a perception layer, then discovering both that the rule stack is hard to extend and that a scaffolded LLM or an imitation policy trained on the bot's own traces has overtaken it.
