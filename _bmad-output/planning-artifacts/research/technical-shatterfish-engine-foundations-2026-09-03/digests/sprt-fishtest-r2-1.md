### Lead 3: Fischer & Ramdas, avoiding overshoot

- CLAIM: arXiv 2410.16076 (Lasse Fischer, Aaditya Ramdas; submitted 2024-10-21, v4 2025-07-08) states that Wald's approximate thresholds (1-beta)/alpha and beta/(1-alpha) "neither guarantee error control at alpha, beta nor optimality" when beta > 0, are conservative when beta = 0, that the looseness is caused by overshoot, and proposes "sequential boosting" (modifying the test statistic to avoid overshoot) which "uniformly improves power-one SPRTs (beta = 0) for simple nulls and alternatives, or for one-sided nulls and alternatives in exponential families" (Bernoulli is an exponential family; the abstract does not name Bernoulli or small p explicitly). | SOURCE: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16076 | PUBLISHER: arXiv | PUB_DATE: 2025-07-08 (v4) | CONFIDENCE: high | CLASS: math

## Contradictions
- Fischer & Ramdas (2410.16076) state Wald's approximate thresholds do not guarantee alpha/beta error control when beta > 0 (overshoot), whereas fishtest/fastchess and Van den Bergh's duration table use exactly those approximate thresholds with alpha = beta = 0.05; fishtest mitigates by clamping the LLR (sprt.py overshoot clamp; normalized_elo_practical.pdf clamp rule), which is a different fix than sequential boosting. Not a factual contradiction, but a design tension the ADR should note.
- Van den Bergh's note redefines terminology: what his earlier "Normalized Elo" note called normalized Elo is now the "normalized t-value", and normalized Elo = C_{e/t} * t_n. Older secondary material may use the earlier meaning.
