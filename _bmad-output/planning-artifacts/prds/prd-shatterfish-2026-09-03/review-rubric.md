# PRD Quality Review — Shatterfish

Calibration: public open-source engine, solo engineer plus product owner, one-user v1, chain-top (feeds architecture, UX for the overlay Panel, and epics). The eight non-negotiables in `docs/BOOTSTRAP-PROMPT.md` section 1 bind the PRD; the review checks that nothing here contradicts them and that the PRD adds what they do not say.

## Overall verdict

This is a real PRD with a thesis, not a backlog with headings: information parity and measurement are stated as disciplines, every feature group serves one of them, the Non-goals do work, the counter-metrics are product-specific, and the Glossary is good enough that the architecture and epics workflows can source-extract from it. What is at risk is done-ness at the rungs the program actually gates on. The E4 rung (FR-31, SM-3) has no number behind "large majority", the Composite outcome the Sequential test optimizes rewards exactly the diving behavior SM-C2 says is worse, and the determinism contract (FR-2) does not say whether a process may host more than one Run, which is the question the E1 classloader spike and the Rig runner both hang on. Fix those three and the PRD is ready for the architecture workflow; the rest is medium-weight hygiene the epics workflow would otherwise have to invent.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions read as decisions. Section 5 says what will not be built and why ("It will not chase turns per second as a product metric; speed serves measurement"); section 6.2 gives each deferral a reason ("deferred until the Harness measures the properties the design depends on"); the addendum records the headline-metric alternatives and why in-game score lost ("gameable"). The Open Questions are genuinely open and each names who answers it and when. The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (section 6.2, coach mode) sits at a real tension: the feature most likely to bring a second user is deferred to E8.

Two tensions the PRD contains but does not call out. First, section 7 and the Glossary disagree about what "better" means for a losing Run (finding below). Second, FR-5's `[ASSUMPTION]` quietly replaces a bootstrap-prompt done-when ("1,000 runs in seconds") with a different E1 exit condition; that is a decision that changes an input the product owner wrote, and it is filed as an assumption rather than surfaced for approval.

### Findings
- **high** Composite outcome contradicts SM-C2 (§ 3 Glossary "Composite outcome"; § 7 SM-C2) — The Glossary orders the per-Run result "win, then depth reached, then turns survived", so among losses a deeper, faster death ranks above a shallower, longer one. SM-C2 says "A Brain that dives and dies deeper is worse" and offers "the Composite outcome and the survival curve" as the corrective, but the Composite outcome is the thing that rewards diving. The Sequential test (FR-21) optimizes this ordering, so the Rig will accept Brains that dive. *Fix:* decide the ordering on purpose and say what was given up. Options: (a) keep depth-first and reword SM-C2 to "depth reached alone, outside the Composite outcome"; (b) rank losses by a survival-weighted measure (depth with a turns floor, or the boss staircase) and note the cost in test power; (c) make the ordering a pre-registered parameter of the Hypothesis ID. Record the choice in the E3 statistics ADR.
- **medium** A bootstrap done-when is rewritten as an assumption (§ 4.1 FR-5; § 14 first entry; § 7 SM-4; § 13 OQ-3) — "the E1 done-when in the bootstrap prompt, '1,000 runs in seconds', is replaced by three measured numbers" changes a binding input from the product owner. One of the three numbers (tactics' leaf correlation and disambiguation) is placed at "E1/E6" by Open Question 3 while SM-4 requires it at E1, and measuring it needs branching from a shared state, which FR-6 defers to E6. *Fix:* convert to a `[NOTE FOR PM]` asking the owner to ratify the new E1 exit condition, and reconcile SM-4 with OQ-3: either the tactics properties are an E1 deliverable with a stated method (replay a seeded Action prefix and branch, since snapshot is deferred), or they move out of SM-4 into the E6 entry condition.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater: section 2 uses jobs-to-be-done for three users and a non-user list, and the personas live in the brief addendum where they belong. The Vision is unswappable ("kills Yog-Dzhewa on a public seed, reproducible from its run log" could only be this product). The NFRs carry thresholds and justifications (NFR-3 gives a target, a minimum, the reason for both, and what changes below the minimum). The Non-goals are specific enough to lose something ("It will not run the bot as a separate process or over a socket"). Section 12 "Why now" is short and asserted from research rather than argued, but it is not furniture; it names a concrete window ("Upstream is quiet while 4.0 is built privately, so the hook surface is stable for months").

No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated in section 1 and every feature group is a consequence of it: Headless engine and Fairness (parity), Codex (knowledge without folklore), Rig (measurement), Brain (observation-driven so a human can take over), Overlay (watch it think). Epic order in section 6.1 follows dependency, not ease. Success metrics validate the thesis rather than activity: SM-1 is the headline, SM-3 to SM-6 are the rungs, and each names the FRs it validates. Counter-metrics exist and are the right ones for this product (SM-C1 turns per second, SM-C3 win rate as an early gate, SM-C4 hook count). MVP scope kind is a platform/measurement capability and the scope logic matches (v1 ends with a debugger Overlay, not a coach).

The one coherence gap is the Composite-outcome finding above, which belongs to this dimension as much as to decision-readiness: the thesis is "nothing about the bot is believed until the rig says so", so what the rig optimizes is the strategy.

### Findings
- **low** UJ-5 and FR-48 to FR-50 have no success metric (§ 7) — Upstream management is the third of the eight non-negotiables and the PRD gives it a journey and three FRs, but nothing in section 7 validates it; SM-C4 only counterbalances it. *Fix:* add a secondary SM ("one upstream tag upgrade lands by FR-50 in a single `touches-upstream` pull request with every Hook re-verified and the Baseline republished") or state that upgrade success is operational and measured by the procedure completing.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Where the PRD writes Consequences blocks (20 of 53 FRs, concentrated in 4.1, 4.2, and the front of 4.4 and 4.5), they are good: "An unidentified item appears in the Observation under its unidentified name only" (FR-3), "A deliberately worse Brain is rejected on the `standard` set" (FR-21), "Interleaving arbitrary human Actions between bot turns never causes an error or a stale plan" (FR-27). Many single-sentence FRs are also testable as written (FR-15, FR-20, FR-22, FR-24, FR-49). The problems are at the rungs the program gates on and at the seams the architecture will build.

### Findings
- **high** "Large majority" is the E4 exit condition and has no number (§ 4.5 FR-31; § 7 SM-3; addendum epic map E4) — FR-31 requires Policies "sufficient to kill Goo on a large majority of the `standard` Seed set" and SM-3 repeats the phrase. E4's done-when is therefore unfalsifiable; the epics workflow cannot write the closing story. *Fix:* state a fraction with a confidence interval (for example "Goo killed on at least 60% of `standard` at 95% confidence, per class where the class is implemented") or, if the owner will not commit before data, make it a `[NOTE FOR PM]` with a placeholder and the moment it is set (the first `standard` run of the random Baseline in E3 is the natural one).
- **high** FR-2 does not say whether a process may run more than one Run (§ 4.1 FR-2; § 4.4 FR-19; § 6.2; § 8 NFR-3) — FR-19 says "One process per Run by default", NFR-3 and FR-5 measure "Runs per minute for one process", and section 6.2 calls classloader isolation "a spike in E1, not a requirement; processes are the default". These agree only if a process hosts exactly one Run, which JVM and libGDX startup makes expensive at 1,000 Runs. If a process hosts Runs sequentially, SPD's static state (`Dungeon`, statistics, journal, RNG) must be reset between Runs and FR-2's "identical Observation hashes" must hold regardless of what ran before in the same process. The PRD does not say which, and the architecture workflow will have to guess. *Fix:* add a Consequence to FR-2: "A Run's Observations are independent of any Run previously executed in the same process" or "The Harness executes at most one Run per process and the Rig's throughput figures include process start". Then make FR-19 and NFR-3 agree with the choice.
- **medium** Pre-registration has no registration mechanism (§ 4.4 FR-22) — "the Rig refuses an unregistered comparison" is testable, but nothing says what a registration is. If it is a local file the developer writes immediately before running, the "pre" is unenforced and a skeptic (UJ-3) cannot verify it. *Fix:* state that registrations are committed files, that a Hypothesis ID is immutable once committed (a changed bound is a new ID), and that a Results page links the commit that introduced its ID.
- **medium** Seed sets have no sizes and `holdout` has no permitted use (§ 4.4 FR-20; § 8 NFR-3; § 10 Cost) — NFR-3's arithmetic assumes "1,000 Runs" for the smoke loop but FR-20 never sizes `smoke`, `standard`, `holdout`, or `bosses`, so the hour budget in section 10 cannot be checked against FR-21's burn-in. `holdout` is "never run during development and its use is recorded" but the PRD never says when it may be run (release? SM-2 publication? once per Baseline?). *Fix:* give each set a size (or a range with the rule for changing it) and state `holdout`'s single permitted use.
- **medium** The Run tuple omits the hero class (§ 3 Glossary "Run"; § 4.1 FR-1; § 8 NFR-2) — A Run is "fully determined by (Upstream tag, seed, Action list)" but FR-1 starts "a new game of a given class and seed". Class is neither an Action nor part of the seed; challenge flags (which FR-14 says parameterize the Codex tables) are in the same position. *Fix:* extend the tuple to (Upstream tag, seed, class, challenge flags, Action list) everywhere it appears (Glossary, FR-2, NFR-2, section 9 Run log format) or state that class and challenges are the first record of the Action list.
- **medium** Save, quit, and resume are unaddressed for the Overlay (§ 4.6 FR-37, FR-40; § 3 Glossary "Run") — The desktop game saves and resumes; a Run is defined "from a fresh start to death, victory, or a turn cap". The PRD does not say whether a resumed game continues the same Run log and Hash chain, whether Beliefs survive a restart, or whether the Overlay refuses loaded games. UX (what the Panel shows on the title screen and on load) and E5 both need this. *Fix:* add a Consequence under FR-37: either "A loaded game continues its Run log; Beliefs are rebuilt from the log on load" or "The Overlay attaches only to games started through the launcher; loading a save ends the Run".
- **low** Adjectives standing in for bounds — FR-1 "fails with a clear message"; FR-26 "the worst deaths" (worst by what: shallowest? largest Evaluation drop? most surprising?); FR-28 "cheap entry predicates"; FR-36 "in a form the Overlay shows"; NFR-4 "the game keeps its frame rate while the Brain thinks" with no floor and no default for the "configurable" thinking budget. *Fix:* one bound each (for FR-26 a definition; for NFR-4 a floor such as "no frame drop below 30 fps attributable to the Brain" and a default budget).

## Scope honesty — strong

Non-goals (section 5) and out-of-scope (section 6.2) are explicit and each carries a reason. Deferrals are inline on the FR ("Deferred to E6; the interface is reserved in E1"), so a reader of section 4 alone cannot mistake FR-6, FR-13, FR-18, FR-33 to FR-35, or FR-44 to FR-47 for v1. The v1/v2 split is stated in the Vision and repeated in 6.2 ("the v1 Overlay is the debugger, v2 is the coach"). Open-items density is right for the stakes: 9 open questions, 6 assumptions, 1 note on a green-light-to-build PRD for a solo engineer, and every open question names its resolution point.

The FR-5 finding above (an input rewritten as an assumption) is the only place where a scope change is softer than it should be.

No further findings.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The Glossary is the PRD's best asset: 40 terms, each with a definition, several with the module or class that owns them, and section 4 uses them consistently in capitalized form. FR-1 to FR-53, UJ-1 to UJ-5, SM-1 to SM-7 and SM-C1 to SM-C5, NFR-1 to NFR-9 are contiguous and unique; every "Realizes UJ-n" and "Validates FR-n" resolves. Section 9 gives the architecture workflow the public surfaces with their versioning rules, and the addendum's epic map and ADR table are what the epics workflow needs. The UX workflow gets the Panel contents (FR-38), the controls (FR-39), the Modes, the map highlights (FR-41), oracle marking (FR-43), the interface-size constraint, and a placement assumption it is told to own.

The gaps are the ones that make a downstream reader go back to another document or invent something.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-50 is in no epic and FR-48 to FR-53 are in no MVP line (§ 6.1; addendum epic map) — The addendum maps E0 to "FR-48, FR-49, FR-51, FR-52, FR-53"; FR-50 (the upgrade procedure, the core of UJ-5) is absent from every epic. Section 6.1's E0 line describes activities rather than FRs, so a reader of the PRD alone cannot find where FR-48 to FR-53 land. *Fix:* put FR-50 in E0 (procedure documented and skill-automated) with its first execution in whichever Epic the 4.0 tag arrives; list FR-48 to FR-53 on section 6.1's E0 line.
- **medium** "A Belief summary" is undefined for the UX workflow (§ 4.6 FR-38; § 3 Glossary "Belief") — Beliefs are "per-unidentified-item candidate sets with probabilities, floor facts, chapter counters, memory of monsters seen and lost". The Panel shows "a Belief summary" and nothing says which of these, in what form, or how many rows. The UX workflow will invent it and FR-32's Decision output will not carry it. *Fix:* name the summary's contents in FR-38 (for example: top candidate per unidentified inventory item with its probability, the chapter counters, the count of monsters known-but-unseen) and state that the Decision (FR-32) carries whatever the Panel shows.
- **low** UJ-3 and UJ-4 have no named protagonist (§ 2.3) — "A community member" and "A player who dies on floor 5" carry their context inline, which is what matters, but the rubric asks for names and the UX workflow will want one for UJ-4 when it reaches E8. *Fix:* name them.
- **low** The methodology page has no FR (§ 2.3 UJ-3; § 4.4 FR-21 Consequence 2; § 4.8 FR-51) — UJ-3 and FR-21 both depend on "the methodology page" (error-rate calibration, what to do when hashes disagree across platforms), but FR-51's list of what the docs site must reach does not include it. *Fix:* add it to FR-51 or as a Consequence of FR-25.
- **low** "Run N" unit (§ 4.6 FR-39) — Step is "one Action"; Run N does not say N Actions or N game turns (a rest or a long move is one Action and many turns). *Fix:* state the unit.

## Shape fit — strong

The PRD is a technical capability spec with one meaningful UX surface, and it is shaped that way: jobs-to-be-done rather than personas, five journeys of which two (UJ-1, UJ-2) are load-bearing, one (UJ-3) defines the reproducibility contract that section 9 formalizes, one is explicitly v2, and one is operational. This is neither over-formalized for a solo builder nor under-formalized for a public artifact whose thesis is that outsiders can check the numbers. Brownfield references (upstream classes, the pinned tag, the six-module layout) are delegated to the bootstrap prompt and the addendum rather than restated, which is right because they must be verified against the tag, not copied. Chain-top traceability (Realizes/Validates, epic map, ADR table) is present without becoming a matrix.

No findings.

## Mechanical notes

- **Glossary drift, section 2.3 versus sections 3 to 11.** The user journeys use lowercase for terms the Glossary capitalizes: "run logs", "results file", "the rig", "the harness", "the panel", "composite outcome", "sequential test", "replay" (UJ-1, UJ-2, UJ-3). UJ-3 also says "SPRT outcome" where the Glossary and FR-21 say "Sequential test" (GSPRT). Harmless to a human, but the epics workflow extracts UJs by term. Suggest capitalizing in 2.3 and replacing "SPRT" with "Sequential test".
- **ID continuity.** FR-1 to FR-53, UJ-1 to UJ-5, SM-1 to SM-7, SM-C1 to SM-C5, NFR-1 to NFR-9: contiguous, no duplicates. Every "Realizes" and "Validates" reference resolves.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip.** Six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (FR-5, FR-29, FR-38, FR-47, SM-2, NFR-3); six index entries in section 14; each pairs. The FR-5 entry should become a `[NOTE FOR PM]` per the decision-readiness finding, which would leave five.
- **Epic map coverage.** All 53 FRs appear in the addendum's map except FR-50 (see finding). FR-6 appears twice by design (E1 interface, E6 realization); the map says so.
- **NFR-2 wording.** "replayable by a third party from Observations only" is not literally possible; a Replay re-runs the engine from (tag, seed, Action list) and checks the Observation hashes. Suggest "verifiable by a third party from the Run log and the pinned tag alone".
- **Required sections for stakes.** Present: vision, target user with non-users, glossary, features with FRs, non-goals, MVP scope, success metrics with counter-metrics, cross-cutting NFRs, public surfaces, constraints, integration, why now, open questions, assumptions index, and an addendum with the epic map, ADR list, and surface sketches. Nothing missing for a chain-top capability spec at this stakes level. A Risks section is deliberately absent ("risks referenced, not repeated" per the addendum); acceptable because the brief holds them, but section 0 should say so.
