# PRD Quality Review — PRD: Shatterfish (version 2, status final)

Reviewed: `prd.md` (v2) and `addendum.md` only. Independent of earlier reviews and validation reports.

## Overall verdict

This is a PRD with a thesis and the discipline to carry it: information parity and measurement are stated as the two bets, every feature is traceable to one of them, the non-goals do real work, the counter-metrics are product-specific, and the FRs mostly come with testable consequences rather than adjectives. It is ready to feed architecture and epics. What is at risk is the statistical spine that the whole program leans on: the E4 gate (SM-3, FR-31) pairs a 75% point target with a 70% lower bound in a way the `standard` set as described cannot satisfy, and reproducing a Results page "byte for byte" (UJ-3) silently requires a Brain determinism guarantee that no FR states. Both are one-paragraph fixes but they sit under the headline claims, so they rank high. A handful of undefined parameters (turn cap, Warrior triple count, "deliberately worse" Brain) should be pinned before E3 stories are cut.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, with what was given up named. The Vision says outright that "Chess search does not transfer" and only "Stockfish's infrastructure and testing culture do". Score is "initially rejected as gameable; admitted only for winning Runs" (addendum, Options considered). Speed is demoted from bootstrap "done when" to counter-metric SM-C1 ("Faster is not better once the Rig is fast enough"). The `[NOTE FOR PM]` at FR-5 replaces a bootstrap promise ("1,000 runs in seconds") with "No throughput number is promised before the measurement", which is the honest move. SM-C4 pre-empts the obvious objection ("why not gate on win rate?") with "Near zero it carries no information; gating on it stalls the program".

The open questions are genuinely open: 1 to 4 and 12 are measurements or code checks assigned to an Epic; 10 and 11 are real forks. Only OQ 5 ("Likely none before E6") and OQ 9 (a lookup) are soft. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` at section 6.2 (coach mode "is the feature most likely to bring a second user") sits at a real tension between the v1 debugger and the v2 coach.

The one weakness is that the assumptions index (section 14) lists "Decisions taken under delegation" as bare outcomes and points to a memlog outside the document for the reasoning. A reader pushing back on the hook budget of eight, or on 75%/70% for Goo, finds the decision but not the rejected alternative.

### Findings
- **medium** Delegated decisions carry no rejected alternative (§14, "Decisions taken under delegation") — Nine decisions are listed as outcomes only ("the hook budget of eight (section 10)", "the Goo threshold of 75% with a 70% lower bound"), and the rationale is delegated to "the memlog", which is not among the frontmatter inputs and not readable from the PRD. The addendum says the brief's addendum "already holds rejected alternatives", but these decisions were taken during the PRD session, so the brief cannot hold them. *Fix:* one line per delegated decision in the addendum naming the alternative considered and why it lost (for example: budget of eight because the expected list in section 10 has six entries plus two spare; 75% because the published symbolic-bot Goo rate is X), or list the memlog as an input.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater: section 2 names three roles (developer, community reader, learner) and one of them is explicitly "v2". Each drives something: the developer drives UJ-1 and the Rig FRs, the community reader drives FR-22 to FR-25 and SM-5, the learner drives FR-47 and the coach-mode note. Section 2.2 non-users are specific ("Players wanting a cheat", "Upstream: Shatterfish proposes nothing").

The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD; "the Observer, the one class that is the only door from game state to the bot" and "nothing about the bot is believed until the Rig says so" are this product. The NFRs are product-shaped: NFR-3 refuses to invent a number ("No rate is promised before it is measured") and instead bounds the thing that matters ("the `smoke` direction check ... fits within a working session ... and the `standard` acceptance run fits overnight"); NFR-2 names the tuple and the cross-platform nightly; NFR-8 is a real commitment (no network calls). Section 12 "Why now" is short but earned from the research inputs ("no bot, RL agent, gym, or headless harness exists for SPD"; "Upstream is quiet while 4.0 is built privately").

The "Provenance:" lines under FRs (FR-17, FR-26, FR-29, FR-33, FR-39, FR-42) are the opposite of furniture: they record why an item moved between Epics, which is exactly what the epics workflow needs.

No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated in section 1 and repeated structurally: parity (section 4.2, NFR-1, section 10 "Safety: information parity ... a release blocker regardless of Results") and measurement (section 4.4, section 7, section 9). The goal ladder ("first beat the final boss, then score as high as possible") maps to SM-1 and SM-2, with SM-7 as "the check on SM-2" and SM-C3 guarding "Score at the cost of wins". The rungs (SM-3 to SM-6) are each tied to an Epic's done-when, and the addendum's epic map reproduces that. The MVP is a platform scope (E0 to E5 build the instrument; the Brain is deliberately a baseline) and section 6.2 says why each later Epic waits ("deferred until the Harness measures the properties the design depends on").

Counter-metrics exist and each names what it counterbalances. Prioritization follows the thesis rather than convenience: the Rig (E3) precedes the Brain (E4) because "no brain change merges without rig numbers".

Two metric-validity gaps sit under the headline and the E4 gate.

### Findings
- **high** SM-3's paired thresholds are not satisfiable on the `standard` set as described (§7 SM-3; FR-31; FR-20) — SM-3 requires "at least 75% of the `standard` Seed set's Warrior triples (lower confidence bound at least 70% at the committed size)". FR-20 fixes `standard` at 500 triples of "(seed, hero class, challenge flags)" but never states the class mix; SM-7 uses the same set for six classes, implying roughly 83 Warrior triples. A 95% Wilson lower bound at 75% observed is about 0.65 at n=83, 0.69 at n=200, and only reaches 0.70 at roughly n=350. So either the gate needs an observed rate near 80% (n=83) or nearly the whole set must be Warrior. The PRD says the sizes are "revisable by ADR once throughput is measured, together with SM-3's bound", but the epics workflow will cut E4's acceptance story from this text as written. *Fix:* state the Warrior triple count in `standard` (or define a Warrior-only subset, or make `bosses` the E4 set) and pick one of: gate on the lower bound alone, or a point target consistent with the count (for example 80% of 100 Warrior triples).
- **medium** SM-1's "fresh set drawn at claim time" path has no attempt bound (§7 SM-1; FR-20; FR-22) — `holdout` may be used "at most once per Brain version", but SM-1 alternatively allows "a fresh set drawn at claim time, with no prior Runs of that Brain on that seed". FR-22's ledger counts prior Runs "for a Brain commit on a Seed set", and a freshly drawn set is a new set each time, so a Brain with a 2% win rate can claim the headline after enough draws with a ledger count of zero on the winning set. The PRD's own SM-C4 reasoning (a near-zero rate carries no information) applies to a single Win as well. *Fix:* make the ledger count per Brain commit across all Seed sets, require the fresh set to be committed before its first Run (a Registration, so FR-22's post-dating check applies), and have the SM-1 Results page state the total Runs of that Brain version before the Win.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the PRD's most careful dimension and mostly holds. The convention in section 4 ("An FR's capability sentence is its acceptance test unless a 'Consequences (testable)' list follows") is applied consistently; 30 of 53 FRs have consequence lists, and the remaining capability sentences are mostly bounded (FR-28 "evaluate without side effects and without simulation"; FR-31 "at least 75% ... lower confidence bound of at least 70%"; FR-32 "at least the top three alternatives"; FR-47 "at most one plain sentence"; FR-38 "200 UI pixels", "1280 by 720 window at interface scale 3 collapses"; FR-39 "Next Step advances exactly one Input wait"). Epic-tagged consequences ("[E5]", "[E4; required for any learned component, E9]") are the right way to keep an FR whole while splitting its stories. The non-functional sections give bounds where they can and say when they cannot (NFR-3).

The gaps are few but they are load-bearing.

### Findings
- **high** Brain determinism is required by UJ-3, SM-5, and FR-21 but stated nowhere (§2.3 UJ-3; §4.5; §8 NFR-2, NFR-4) — FR-2 and NFR-2 make a Run "fully determined by (Upstream tag, hero class, challenge flags, seed, Action list)", which is a Harness guarantee given an Action list. UJ-3 promises more: a skeptic "run[s] the command, and get[s] the same per-seed results and the same Hash chain", i.e. the Brain regenerates the same Action list. That needs the Brain to be a deterministic function of (Observation sequence, configuration, Brain seed) with tie-breaking and Belief sampling seeded, and it needs Rig think budgets in nodes or Input waits rather than wall-clock. NFR-4 instead defines "a per-Input-wait thinking budget" that the Panel reports when "exceeded", which is time-based, and OQ 5 leaves budgets open. Redetermination (FR-6, Glossary Search) samples "from Beliefs", with no statement of what seeds the sampler. Without this, "byte for byte" fails the first time a Policy breaks a tie or Search runs. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-27 (or a new FR in 4.5): the Brain is deterministic given the Observation sequence, its configuration, and a Brain seed recorded in the Run log header; any randomness in the Brain draws from that seed only; Rig budgets are counted in Input waits or nodes, never time; the Overlay's time budget is a display concern only. Add "Brain seed" to the FR-25 Results page and the FR-23 Run log.
- **medium** "A deliberately worse Brain" is undefined (FR-21 consequences; §7 SM-5) — The calibration test for the Sequential test is "A deliberately worse Brain is rejected on the `standard` set", and SM-5 gates E3 on it. A random agent is trivially worse and proves little about the test's sensitivity; a Brain with one Policy removed might be a few percent worse and be the actual thing the test must detect. The effect size the Rig must resolve is the single most important parameter of the E3 statistics ADR and it is not stated. *Fix:* name the degraded Brains (for example the Baseline with the heal Policy disabled, and the Baseline with Evaluation weights perturbed by a stated amount) and state the expected verdict for each; leave the bounds to the ADR.
- **medium** The turn cap is undefined (Glossary "Run"; FR-19; FR-20; NFR-3) — A Run ends at "death, Win, or a turn cap"; the cap bounds the overnight budget in NFR-3, is a term in the Composite outcome ("then turns survived"), and changes what "cause of death" means for a capped Run. No value, no owner, and no place (Seed set file? Registration?) is given. *Fix:* make the cap part of the Seed set file or the Registration so it is versioned with the comparison, and have FR-19's per-Run results record "capped" as a distinct outcome.
- **low** Two unbounded adjectives in otherwise bounded text (FR-42 Provenance; §9 Run log format) — FR-42 hinges v1 hotkeys on whether "the binding Hook is small" with no threshold; section 9 wants "a clear message" for an incompatible Run log. *Fix:* FR-42: "at most N lines in at most one upstream file"; section 9: the message names both schema versions.
- **low** NFR-4 has no number (§8 NFR-4) — "the game keeps its frame rate while the Brain thinks" is testable as a comparison but no tolerance is given. *Fix:* "frame time on the render thread does not change by more than X ms at the 95th percentile with the Brain thinking versus idle", measured in the E5 story.
- **low** FR-17's "every mechanics claim ... the docs rely on" is checkable on the Brain side only (FR-17) — The PRD makes the Brain's set enumerable ("the Brain's own Rules index so 'every' is checkable") and the citation checker catches citations that "no longer resolve", but nothing detects an uncited claim in the docs. *Fix:* either narrow the sentence to `docs/rules/` and the Brain index, or add a docs lint that flags mechanics statements outside those files (accepting that this is heuristic).

## Scope honesty — adequate

Section 5 is a real Non-goals section: ten entries, each of which closes a door someone would otherwise walk through ("will not reimplement any game rule in any language for play", "will not chase Input waits per second as a product metric", "will not train a learned Evaluation before the hand-tuned one has a measured Baseline"). Section 6.2 de-scopes with reasons and section 6.1 lists exactly which FRs and which tagged consequences each Epic owns. Deferrals are inline on the FR ("Deferred to E6; the interface is reserved in E1") rather than discovered later. The classloader spike is explicitly "not a requirement". The `[ASSUMPTION]` index is empty by design and the PRD says why, and the Tier 3 discipline (every game claim is a hypothesis until cited, collected in OQ 12) is a strong form of scope honesty for a brownfield PRD.

Open-items density: 12 open questions, 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, 0 `[ASSUMPTION]` on a green-light PRD for a multi-Epic program. That is lean, and mostly because the questions are measurement questions assigned to Epics rather than unresolved product decisions. Two things are assumed silently.

### Findings
- **medium** A fresh Profile per Run assumes every hero class is available in a fresh Profile, and conflicts with the learner's expectation (Glossary "Profile"; FR-1; FR-20; SM-7; UJ-4; FR-37) — The Glossary says "Every Run, headless or embedded, gets its own fresh Profile", and SM-7 runs "Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Huntress, Duelist, Cleric" from `standard`. If the game gates classes behind badges or unlocks in the Profile (Tier 3; not in OQ 12's list), the Harness needs a way to start a locked class in a fresh Profile, which is either a Hook against the budget of eight or a Profile-seeding step nobody has specified. On the Overlay side, UJ-4's learner "turns on coach mode" and presumably expects their own badges, journal, and unlocks; the fresh-Profile rule silently takes those away, and FR-37 confirms "A save opened without the launcher is not an Overlay Run". *Fix:* add class availability in a fresh Profile to OQ 12; state in FR-1 how a class the Profile would lock is started; add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at UJ-4 or FR-47 that coach mode on the player's own Profile is a v2 decision with a parity cost (journal catalog is per-Profile knowledge).
- **low** OQ 6 refers to a target the PRD does not define (§13 OQ 6) — "Which human win-rate source calibrates 'beats the median human'?" is the only occurrence of that phrase; no SM or FR sets a beats-the-median-human target. Either it belongs to the brief's ladder and should be cited as such, or it is a leftover. *Fix:* cite the brief's rung or delete the question.

## Downstream usability — strong

This is a chain-top PRD (section 0 names architecture, UX, and epics as consumers) so this dimension matters, and it is built for it. The Glossary is large (about 55 terms in four groups) and the FRs use those terms with their glossary capitalization throughout (Input wait, Observation, Decision, Run log, Hash chain, Seed set, Registration, Composite outcome, Per-pair statistic). IDs are contiguous and unique: FR-1 to FR-53, UJ-1 to UJ-5, SM-1 to SM-8, SM-C1 to SM-C6, NFR-1 to NFR-9, OQ 1 to 12. Every "open question N" reference in the body resolves (3, 4, 7, 10, 11, 12), and every "section N" reference points at an existing section. Every SM names the FRs it validates and every feature description names the UJs it realizes, so the epics workflow can build the traceability matrix from the text alone. The addendum's requirement-to-epic map and mechanism-decisions table are exactly what the architecture workflow needs and are explicit that they are "non-binding; the ADRs decide". Sections pull out cleanly; section 9 (public surfaces) and section 10 (constraints) can be handed to architecture on their own.

Minor drift and one sync gap are listed under Mechanical notes; none of it drives the verdict.

No findings.

## Shape fit — strong

The product is a technical capability spec (harness, rig, codex, fairness) with one human-facing surface (the Overlay) and one skeptical outside reader, built by a solo engineer on a brownfield fork. The PRD's shape matches: capability FRs with testable consequences carry the weight; five UJs, no more, with the one v2 journey marked as "Lighter scope"; SMs that are operational rungs plus two program-level headlines; a section 9 on public surfaces and versioning that a consumer-product PRD would not need but this one does. The UX detail is correctly delegated ("the FRs below bind the capabilities and the spine binds the detail") rather than duplicated. Brownfield handling is disciplined: every claim about SPD's existing behavior is tagged "(Tier 3)" and collected in OQ 12 for verification against the pinned code, and section 11 pins the second upstream source for the vocabulary diff. Existing-code references are not asserted as fact anywhere.

No findings.

## Mechanical notes

- **Addendum term drift.** The PRD defines "Input wait" and uses "Input waits per second" (FR-5, SM-4, SM-C1); the addendum says "Turns per second" in the E1 epic-map row and "turns per second (a Rig property, now a counter-metric)" under Options considered. Align to "Input waits per second".
- **Epic map out of sync with section 6.1.** The addendum says the map is "Derived from PRD section 6.1 ... keep the two in sync", but 6.1's E4 row includes "the E4-tagged consequences of FR-9" and its E5 row includes "the E5-tagged consequences of FR-4, FR-11, FR-12, FR-23, FR-27, FR-36"; the addendum's E4 and E5 rows omit both.
- **`DESIGN.md` cited as an input but not listed.** Section 0 says the Overlay spine is "`EXPERIENCE.md` and `DESIGN.md`, listed in the inputs"; the frontmatter `inputs` lists only `EXPERIENCE.md`. FR-38 also cites "the spine's Layout section" without saying which file. Add `DESIGN.md` to `inputs`.
- **Tier 3 roundtrip.** Section 14 says Tier 3 statements are "collected in open question 12". FR-2's "(Tier 3)" claim ("the general-purpose generator used for combat rolls") is not in OQ 12's list; OQ 4 covers it only indirectly. The other Tier 3 marks (Floor, Win, Score, Composite outcome, FR-38 camera offset, SM-7 class list) all appear in OQ 12.
- **`[NOTE FOR PM]` index.** Section 14 lists two callouts (FR-5, section 6.2); exactly two exist in the body. Roundtrip is clean. `[ASSUMPTION]` count is zero as stated.
- **UJ protagonists.** UJ-2 has a named protagonist ("Bash"); UJ-1, UJ-3, UJ-4, UJ-5 use roles ("The developer", "A community member", "A learner"). Acceptable for a solo-developer capability spec; if the UX workflow wants names for UJ-3 and UJ-4, they are the two that carry an outside reader's context.
- **"Explain" appears twice with different meanings.** FR-39 defines an "Explain" control in v1 (expand the Decision to Policy, alternatives, Safety flags); FR-44 is "Explain view (v2)" (Evaluation terms, Search outcomes). Consider "Explain" (v1 control) and "Explain, extended" or a distinct name for FR-44 so stories do not collide.
- **Required sections.** Vision, users and UJs, Glossary, FRs, Non-goals, MVP scope, SMs with counter-metrics, NFRs, public surfaces, constraints, integrations, Why now, Open questions, Assumptions index: all present. The addendum carries the epic map, deferred mechanism decisions, surface sketches, metric options, and an inputs-landed table.
